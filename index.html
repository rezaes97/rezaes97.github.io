01 / About

Learning beyond the training distribution.

Practical computer vision systems often face two linked challenges: few labeled examples for a new task and distribution shifts between training and deployment. I am interested in methods that make pretrained visual models more adaptable under these constraints. This includes learning useful domain representations, updating only a small part of a model, and evaluating how well visual features transfer to unfamiliar classes and environments.

02 / Research interests

Questions that guide my work.

My interests sit at the intersection of representation learning, model adaptation, and dependable visual recognition.

01

Few-shot & cross-domain learning

How can a model recognize novel classes when only a handful of labeled examples are available and the target domain differs from its training data?

02

Parameter-efficient adaptation

How can pretrained vision transformers be specialized through lightweight updates while retaining transferable visual representations?

03

Visual anomaly detection

How can feature-space models identify local defects and structural deviations, particularly when anomalous training examples are scarce?

03 / Publication

Selected research.

A peer-reviewed study addressing data scarcity and domain shift in in-field plant disease recognition.

Computers and Electronics in Agriculture 2026 · Volume 248 · Article 111691

Dual-stage parameter-efficient tuning for in-field plant disease few-shot classification

Saeed Khankalantary and Mohammad Reza Eskandari

This paper introduces a two-stage parameter-efficient framework for cross-domain few-shot plant disease recognition. First, offline domain adaptation uses domain-relevant plant imagery to improve the visual representation. Then, online few-shot adaptation specializes the model to a target task using a limited labeled support set. Experiments evaluate the approach on PlantDoc, PlantSeg, and PlantWild.
